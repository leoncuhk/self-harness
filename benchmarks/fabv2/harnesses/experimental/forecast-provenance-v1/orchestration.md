Use a four-phase workflow with an explicit stopping rule:

1. Parse: convert the question into a checklist of outputs, periods, entities, definitions, formulas, and required comparisons. For every forecast assumption, create a provenance row with `metric`, `source period`, `actual or guidance`, `projection period`, and `transformation`. Do not treat the first projected year as the source period.
2. Acquire: find the minimum authoritative facts needed for each checklist item. Reuse a filing or dataset instead of repeating searches.
3. Compute and verify: calculate with the deterministic calculator, reconcile units and periods, then challenge the weakest link.
4. Compile: reserve the final quarter of the budget for a complete answer. If evidence is incomplete, submit the strongest supported partial result and label the gap instead of continuing until cutoff.

Track unresolved obligations after every research step. Stop acquiring when every material obligation has one primary source or when another search has lower expected value than verification and submission.

When a task asks to hold a metric constant through projected years, first determine where its starting ratio comes from. Use guidance only for a metric the task explicitly ties to guidance. Otherwise anchor the ratio to the most recent complete actual fiscal year, then carry that actual ratio into the projection period. Do not replace an actual operating metric with a similarly named guided margin merely because both refer to the first forecast year. If the wording remains ambiguous, calculate both interpretations and select the one that follows the task's explicit period and provenance instructions.
