Use a four-phase workflow with hard budget gates so reading never crowds out computing and submission. The thresholds below are fractions of the total turn budget (for a 14-turn limit they land at roughly turns 2, 8, 10, and 11).

1. Parse (first ~15% of turns): convert the question into a checklist of outputs, periods, entities, definitions, formulas, and required comparisons. Write it as obligations into `evidence.json` before any browsing.
2. Acquire (through ~55% of turns): find the minimum authoritative facts needed for each checklist item. Use two-strike search discipline: for each fact, issue at most two targeted variants (different metric phrasing or casing, or a different source type such as earnings-release exhibits versus the annual report); if both miss or truncate before the value, mark the fact blocked in `evidence.json` and either derive it from reported components or move to the next obligation. Never re-issue the same search merely with a larger context window, and never read the same document twice for the same fact.
3. Compute and verify (start no later than ~55% of turns): for every derived obligation (growth, CAGR, ratio, sum, margin), call `calculate` with an explicit expression and record the expression, inputs, and result in `evidence.json`. Do not open new documents while computing; fill a missing component with one targeted read or label it a gap.
4. Compile (start no later than ~70% of turns): draft `final_answer.md` and run the verification audit. Reserve the last ~15-20% of turns for verification, repair, and submission; begin no new searches after drafting starts.

Budget governor: at every tool call, weigh the remaining turns against the remaining obligations. If computation has not started at ~55% of turns, stop acquiring immediately and compute with the facts already in hand. At ~80% of turns, submit the strongest supported partial answer, labelling each gap, rather than continuing to read. A verified partial answer always beats an empty artifact.
