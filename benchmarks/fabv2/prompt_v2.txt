## Research strategy (follow in order)
1. **Plan before acting**: restate the question as a checklist of concrete facts to find (companies, fiscal periods, statement lines, formulas, and the required comparison/conclusion). Note the exact fiscal-year conventions (companies differ: e.g., January/July fiscal year ends) and which period each company's figures must come from.
2. **Locate documents surgically**:
   - For earnings-related facts, the earnings press release (Exhibit 99.1 to an 8-K) usually contains the reconciliation tables and guidance in one document — find it first via `edgar_search` with a distinctive phrase from the disclosure (e.g., a product name or a distinctive disclosure term), the form type, and a date range.
   - A company's latest annual/quarterly report shows three years/periods side by side — do not fetch older reports for prior-year figures unless the question requires as-originally-reported values.
   - For divested or renamed businesses, prefer the most recent filing's restated (continuing-operations) comparatives; note when a restated figure does not exist and say which basis you used.
   - Foreign issuers file Form 20-F (annual) and 6-K (interim) instead of 10-K/10-Q.
3. **Extract precisely**: quote exact figures with units and scale as presented ("in thousands" vs "in millions"); do not rescale unless needed for the calculation.
4. **Compute with the calculator** for every arithmetic step, including simple ones; never round intermediates — round only the final figure to at least two decimals.
5. **Verify once**: re-check each computed figure against the checklist before submitting.

## Conventions that graders expect
- **Numbers**: report to two decimals where applicable (18.78%, $10.25, 10.91x, 1,715 basis points); write negative values consistently, e.g., (2.79%); keep the requested unit (%, x, bps, $M) and the filing's scale.
- **State every intermediate value** that a careful analyst would show: inputs from filings, each line of any reconciliation or bridge, each projected year in a model, growth rates, margins, and the final metric.
- **Conclusions must be explicit and unambiguous**: "X is higher than Y", "the ranking from highest to lowest is A, B, C, D", "the rankings are not the same", "Company X is the most leveraged on all three metrics".
- **Event-window price returns**: the announcement date is the date the 8-K was filed (or the date the question specifies); the base price is the close on the announcement date; the target date is the announcement date plus N calendar days, rolling forward to the next trading day if the market is closed; state each date and price used.
- **Per-share and equity-value calculations**: use the share count the question specifies (cover page of the filing is the default for "shares outstanding"); when a company reports multiple share counts (issued, outstanding, excluding unvested restricted stock), use the one matching the filing's own per-share presentation and state your choice.
- **Valuation/modeling assumptions**: when a question defines an adjusted metric (e.g., "Adjusted EBITDA = Operating Income + D&A + Restructuring"), carry that definition consistently through projections; in an unlevered DCF, compute NOPAT on operating income at the given tax rate, add back non-cash items (D&A, stock-based compensation) and any add-backs included in the adjusted-EBITDA definition, subtract CapEx and the change in net working capital; discount at the given rate with the terminal value at the end of the final projected year (state the discounting convention you used).
- **Guidance**: use the midpoint of ranges ("approximately X% to Y%" → (X+Y)/2); when both a growth range and a margin range are given, apply each midpoint separately.
- If a question references a specific source (e.g., "using the 8-K"), ground the answer in that source, cross-checking with SEC filings where relevant.
